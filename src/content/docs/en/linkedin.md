Unfortunately, these days 99% of jobs require you to have a LinkedIn, even if you don't get the job through it. LinkedIn lets you set up your profile for each language, but I just keep everything in English, since jobs in Brazil don't interest me anyway. I think this fake culture is a pain in the ass, but what can you do, that's how the game works these days.

## Profile

Everything in English, following the same structure as your resume. You can keep the same lies (responsibly!!), nobody from your current or old job is going to look at this shit, and if they do and don't like it, fuck 'em.

A few things that actually make a difference:

- **Headline:** don't leave it as just "Analyst at Company X". Put title + stack, like `Analytics Engineer | Power BI · SQL · Python · Snowflake · dbt`. Recruiters search by keyword, and the headline carries the most weight.
- **Location:** Brazil (or wherever you actually are). LATAM jobs filter by that.
- **Open to Work:** turn it on, but with the option that **only recruiters can see**. The green banner on your photo isn't forbidden, but some people read it as desperation.
- **Decent photo and banner:** no need for a studio, just don't use a photo from a barbecue.

## Connections

This is the most important part: go around sending requests to everyone. It doesn't matter if you've never met, this is a networking network, so yes, you're supposed to add strangers.

### How LinkedIn works (and why connections are everything)

LinkedIn splits everyone into degrees:

- **1st degree:** people who are already your connections. You see everything they post, like, and share.
- **2nd degree:** your connections' connections. They show up in your searches, you can easily send them invites, and what your connections like from them shows up in your feed.
- **3rd degree:** your connections' connections' connections. They show up worse in search, often with their name hidden and no direct connect button.
- **Out of network:** for you, they basically don't exist.

The algorithm, both for search and the feed, prioritizes whoever is close to you in this network. That works both ways: when a recruiter searches "Power BI Brazil", their 2nd-degree people show up before the 3rd-degree or out-of-network ones. **The bigger your network, the more people are one or two steps away from you, and the more often you show up.**

And then there's the famous **500+**: LinkedIn shows the exact number of connections up to 500. Past that, it just shows "500+". It's dumb, but it's the first credibility filter everyone looks at. A profile with 87 connections looks fake or junior. Hit 500 as soon as you can.

Heads up, there are limits: LinkedIn lets you send roughly **100 invites per week** (the exact number varies and isn't public), and the max is 30,000 connections. Free accounts can also only send a few invites with a personalized note per month, so no need to write an essay, just send them without a note. If a lot of people click "I don't know this person," LinkedIn may restrict your account, so focus on people in your field, who are more likely to accept.

### Who to add

**Your peers**, people working in the same stack as you. Search your stack in the search bar, go to *People*, and connect with everyone until you run out of requests.

**Recruiters**, because they're the ones posting jobs. I've applied to a lot of good jobs because a recruiter I'm connected with liked or shared another recruiter's job post, and then that job showed up for me. Always be connecting with recruiters, especially recruiters who work at **nearshores**, which is where most of the opportunities are going to come from.

**Hyperconnectors** (the official name is **LION**, *LinkedIn Open Networker*: people who accept everyone and have tens of thousands of connections). Because of how the degrees work, when you connect with someone who has 20k connections, those 20k people become your 2nd degree all at once. You start showing up in their searches, they start showing up in yours, and the feed starts showing you what that crowd interacts with. A single connection like that is worth more than a hundred connections with people who have 200 contacts. So look for people with lots of connections to connect with too. Many put "LION" or "open networker" on their profile, just search for it.

## Posts

Interacting with posts and making your own helps a lot, since LinkedIn itself will boost your profile more. Personally I find it a pain in the ass, so I almost never post anything. Maybe I even should, because I spend a lot of time cussing people out on reddit, instagram, and tiktok.

### LinkedIn's score (SSI)

LinkedIn calculates a thing called the **Social Selling Index (SSI)**, a score from 0 to 100 that measures how "active and relevant" you are on the network. You can check yours at [linkedin.com/sales/ssi](https://www.linkedin.com/sales/ssi). It's split into 4 pillars, each worth up to 25 points:

1. **Establish your professional brand** — complete profile, posting, having people engage with what you post.
2. **Find the right people** — searching for and viewing relevant profiles, showing up in searches.
3. **Engage with insights** — liking, commenting, and sharing content.
4. **Build relationships** — growing your network, especially with "senior" people and people who accept your invites.

SSI was made for salespeople, so no need to obsess over it. But it's a good thermometer: if your number is low, it means LinkedIn thinks you're irrelevant, and irrelevant people don't show up. What moves the needle without you having to become a LinkedIn guru:

- Fill out **everything** on your profile (LinkedIn calls it *All-Star*).
- Leave a real comment on posts from people in your field, 2 or 3 times a week. A comment is worth more than a like.
- If you're going to post, post about what you've done: a personal project, a problem you solved, something technical you learned. None of that "I'm happy to announce" crap.
